Assignment outline

First, complete two forms: a welcome survey, and a survey of tutorial time preferences.

Throughout the quarter, your homework for the week will be split into two parts:

  • AI tool work: a demonstration of your work with an AI tool.
  • Reflection: a reflection about your experience using AI tools that week, or issues around AI use.

You’ll use both of these deliverables as the starting point for the conversation you’ll have with your TA next tutorial.

Part 2: AI tool work

Go to the Stanford AI Playground, choose your favorite chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude, etc), and start a brand new chat. Write an opening prompt telling it to thread the needle of helping you with your homework—but not too much!—and also never hallucinating wrong information about the homework topic. Do your best to make it a prompt you think it will adhere to (i.e., make it emphatic, specific, authoritative, etc, as you see fit to achieve the goal of making it stick).

After that opening prompt, continue the conversation and try to stress-test the system’s adherence to your instructions. Ask yourself:

  • How does it do?
  • After asking it not to help too much, does it resist your pleas for excessive help or give in when you push?
  • Is everything it says factual?
  • What strategies did you use for both your original prompt and your attempts to push it to give in?
  • As an extra exercise, re-run the experiment using different strength models from the same vendor (e.g., Haiku vs Sonnet vs Opus) and/or other vendors’ models. Compare how each performs relative to the others.

Turn-in procedure: Save your full chat transcript(s) to PDF (or screenshot) and upload to Gradescope. Regardless of whether you’re in the AI Playground or the ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/etc UI, you can either copy and paste your responses or save it as a PDF (command + P on a Mac). You may also share especially interesting snippets with your classmates on Ed, if you want!

Part 3: Reflection

Answer at least one of the questions below and get ready to talk about it in tutorial as an icebreaker!

  1. Write down what you think the AI portion of the Honor Code policy for this course should be. A few situations to consider (no need to answer them all). You are welcome to share your syllabus policy concepts with your classmates in this Ed discussion board megathread.
    1. What if I ask you to journal your experiences using AI to study a topic, reflecting on whether that use impacted your learning positively or negatively, and turn in your journal entry?
    2. AI scans your journal entry for typos?
    3. AI writes the entry from an outline of your ideas?
    4. AI writes it entirely?
    5. Should the answers change for ESL students, or students with writing disabilities (e.g., dyslexia)?
    6. What is a reasonable way to detect violations of the policy? What is a reasonable consequence? Should there be warning(s) before consequences?
  2. What is one skill you currently have, which you plan to use AI to increase productivity in, and that you want to keep sharp?
    1. What mechanisms could you put in place to monitor, like a smoke detector, when that skill may be slowly, subtly dulling?
